the heat leaves quietly, and then one evening you step outside and the air has teeth again.

it happens around late october in the hills. you don't notice the transition while it's taking place. you just wake up, reach for an extra layer without thinking, and walk out into a morning that smells like dry pine and woodsmoke. the sun is still out, but it isn’t heavy anymore. it doesn’t demand that you sit inside and wait it out. it just hangs there, pale and clean, lighting up the dust on the road.

there is an appetite that arrives with this cold.

in summer, you survive the days. you drink water, you look for a fan, you move from one shaded corner to another, trying not to let the afternoon exhaust you. but the moment the temperature drops, you stop trying to protect yourself from the hours. you want to devour them. you walk faster. you stay out late with the boys, hands around a paper cup of boiling chai just to steal the heat, laughing about things that aren’t even particularly funny. the food tastes sharper. the roads feel wider. even the silence at midnight feels like something you want to fill your lungs with instead of hide from.

i spent a lot of months writing about the things that cost me. about the rooms that felt too small, the weight i was carrying, the quiet ways people hurt you without meaning to. and all of that was true when i wrote it. i don't take any of it back.

but there is another side to feeling everything that nobody mentions when they want to be poetic about sorrow.

when you are built to feel things deeply, you don't just drown in the dark. you devour the good things too. you feel the cold air hit your collarbone and it wakes up every nerve you forgot you had. you look at the sunset over the ridge, the sky turning that bruised violet color before it goes black, and you don’t have to turn it into a metaphor. you just stand there on the side of the road, cold in your sneakers, completely alive, completely full.

the winter will come, and it will get harsher. the college will end, the bags will be packed, and everyone will scatter to different cities. all of that is waiting down the road.

but not tonight. tonight the tea is hot, the air is clean, and for the first time in a long time, i don't need the world to be any different than it is.

— K.S.
