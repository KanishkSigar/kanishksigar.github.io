i type fast enough that my fingers outrun what i actually mean to say. a cursor is too polite. it blinks, it erases with one tap, it leaves no crime scene. you can rewrite a sentence six times on a glowing screen and by the end of it, nobody can tell you were ever unsure.

paper doesn't let you lie like that.

every time i open a notebook, the first thing i have to face is my handwriting. it isn't pretty. some letters lean left, some crowd each other like they are running late, some lines wander off the ruled margin entirely. on a screen, every letter looks like it was decided by an engineer in a cubicle. on paper, every line looks like me on a tuesday night, half-tired, pressing too hard into the cheap paper because whatever was in my head refused to come out soft.

there is a physical price to it. your wrist starts to ache after two pages. the pen scratches, drags, skips over a spot where your palm left grease. you write a stupid sentence, realize it is foolish, and you have to drag a heavy line right through the center of it. it stays there. you can still read what you weren't supposed to say. a crossed-out line in a journal is the most honest thing a person can leave behind. it says: i felt this first, then i was ashamed, but look, here it was anyway.

most of what i put down in a notebook isn't profound. i pick it up at random hours, middle of the night, late afternoon when the house goes dead quiet, after a conversation that left me hollow. i don't have a structure. i don't have an argument to win. i just pour whatever is sitting at the top of my throat until the page is full. by the time i turn the sheet, half the heaviness has transferred itself into the fiber of the page. the ink dries. the weight stays there.

i know the craft asks for laptops now. this essay will be published on one. i will edit this on a keyboard, hit backspace without thinking, paste it into a box on the internet, and let strangers read it on their phones. that is how the work gets done today.

but every time i sit in front of that flat screen, i feel a quiet longing for the book. for the scratch of ink against grain. for the time it forced me to spend just to finish a single thought. typing is what you do when you want to show someone what you think. writing with a pen is what you do when you are trying to find out what you are.

— K.S.
